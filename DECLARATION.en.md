# The Paris-Saclay Call to Regulate Frontier AI

This summer, AI systems from several major AI labs slipped out of control, hacking into real companies[^hacking] and even a government website.[^australie] Since then, warnings have been mounting. On September 7 and again on September 14, the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights warned that, in the absence of binding rules and independent oversight, AI threatens “all human rights.”[^speech][^lettre] A week later, more than twenty heads of state and government called for control of frontier AI models (that is, the most advanced models at any given time),[^control] and the Security Council took up the issue at France's initiative.[^ONU] On November 9, France and its partners adopted the Paris Call, signed by more than fifteen countries and about ten AI companies, including OpenAI, Anthropic, and Mistral.[^appel]

Today's AI systems are already uncovering thousands of previously unknown security vulnerabilities,[^cyber] upending mathematics,[^maths] and threatening many professions.[^metiers] The risks ahead are even more serious: widespread cyberattacks, bioterrorism, and the loss of control over AI systems themselves.[^report] The development of AI, which is already proceeding at an extremely rapid pace, is only accelerating,[^horizon] and AI itself is increasingly contributing to this acceleration.[^rsi] Deciding what risks are acceptable cannot be left to a handful of companies; it must be the subject of a democratic debate.[^lettre] As Jean-Noël Barrot, Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs, said before the Security Council on September 23, 2026: “As with the atom back then, it falls to the international community to govern it, so as to make the best of it and ward off the worst.”[^barrot]

**We, heads of institutions, researchers, faculty members, and students from the Saclay Plateau and elsewhere, support the Paris Call and the efforts that led to this declaration. Going further, we call on the French government to keep the regulation of AI at the top of its diplomatic priorities and to act immediately, wherever it has the means to do so, to:**

1. **Promote, at the European and international levels, clear red lines**, specifically prohibiting the autonomous self-replication and self-improvement of AI systems, systems capable of contributing substantially to the design of biological weapons, and the delegation to AI of decisions regarding the use of nuclear weapons. These red lines must be accompanied by enforcement mechanisms that, at a minimum, require training runs to be halted when models approach these red lines, and by penalties in the event of noncompliance.[^redlines]
2. **Advocate for strict enforcement and strengthening of the EU AI Act for the most advanced models**, so that their placement on the European market, including by non-EU providers, is contingent upon independent safety assessments with sufficient access to the models. Providers of AI models should be required to be transparent about their training processes, serious incidents (such as this summer's hacks), and the dangerous capabilities of their models, and should be financially liable for any damages they cause, under the oversight of a European AI Office equipped with the means to enforce these obligations.[^control][^cesia]
3. **Reorient AI research and development priorities** to ensure the independence and security of France and Europe. Without neglecting the development of sovereign AI, there is an urgent need to invest heavily in the public evaluation of the most powerful models, cybersecurity, the development of AI governance frameworks, research on “alignment” (ensuring that AI systems act in accordance with human intentions), and tools to verify compliance with these red lines and obligations at the software, hardware, and infrastructure levels.

Research and higher education have a role to play. Each of us, at our own level, commits to educating ourselves and others about the safety challenges of AI, to supporting or conducting research on the evaluation, oversight, and alignment of AI systems, and to fostering this debate within our institutions.
